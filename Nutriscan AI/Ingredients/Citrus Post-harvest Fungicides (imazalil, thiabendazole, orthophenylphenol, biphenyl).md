# Citrus Post-harvest Fungicides (imazalil, thiabendazole, orthophenylphenol, biphenyl)

**Category:** preservative  ·  **Function:** Anti-mould treatment on the peel of citrus and bananas after harvest
**Evidence strength:** LIMITED
**E-number(s):** e230, e231, e232, e233
**ADI:** Thiabendazole 0–0.1 mg/kg bw (JMPR 2006); imazalil 0.025 mg/kg bw (EU peer review), 0–0.03 (JMPR)

## Regulatory status
No longer EU food additives (E230–E233 deleted; Dir. 2003/114/EC) and regulated instead as pesticide residues with MRLs (Reg. (EC) 396/2005); EPA pesticide tolerances in the US; sodium o-phenylphenate IARC Group 2B

## Assessment
Fungicides sprayed or waxed onto citrus peel to stop mould in storage, which is why loose lemons are labelled 'waxed, treated with imazalil'. Residues sit on the skin and stay within legal limits in the flesh. IARC classes the sodium salt of orthophenylphenol as possibly carcinogenic (Group 2B, rat bladder tumours). Buy unwaxed fruit when you plan to use the zest.

## Population-specific concerns
- anyone using the peel (zest, marmalade, candied peel)
